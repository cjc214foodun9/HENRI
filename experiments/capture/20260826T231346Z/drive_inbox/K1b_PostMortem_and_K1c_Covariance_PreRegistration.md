The mathematical root cause is **Information Loss via Mean Pooling**:

Let $H = [h_1, h_2, \dots, h_T] \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$ be the sequence of hidden states generated by the Qwen backbone for a task prompt of length $T$. Mean-pooling maps $H \mapsto \bar{h} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$\bar{h} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T h_t$$

**Theorem (First-Order Information Compression Limit):** Mean pooling preserves only the first moment (centroid) of the sequence distribution $P(h)$. Information regarding token order, spatial adjacency, and cross-token relational variance is projected into the kernel null space:

$$I(H; \bar{h}) \ll I(H; H)$$

$$\left\| \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}}{\partial h_t} \right\|_2 = \frac{1}{T} \left\| \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}}{\partial \bar{h}} \right\|_2 \xrightarrow{T \gg 1} 0$$

Because $\frac{1}{T} \to 0$ for $T = 512$ sequence tokens, the policy network receives zero directional gradient information regarding token-level spatial structure ("gradient starvation").
