## The mechanism, measured

A **random direction** scores AUC 0.6629 in the wave channel — nearly equal to the
*compiled* direction's 0.6659. That is the diagnostic: when one class is concentrated
and the other dispersed, almost **any** direction separates them.

Cause: the positive class is generated by a small synonym resource. With 64 templates
but only **4 usable synonym slots** — `move↔push`, `move↔pull`, `push↔pull`,
`grasp↔place` — the positive delta distribution collapses onto ~10 effective
directions. Negatives are drawn from a larger pool and spread over ~23. Class
diversity is therefore mismatched, and **matching byte distance cannot repair a
mismatch in class diversity** — the two are independent properties.

Note v3 drew its motif pool from `['grasp', 'move', 'push']` only: `block↔brick`
dropped out because `brick` is not in the 156-word manifest. The resource is even
smaller than it first appears.

## The boundary this establishes

**Online compilation from demonstration pairs cannot be evaluated on the local
resource set.** The mechanism is implemented and runs, but any synonym swap drawn
from this vocabulary produces a degenerate positive class, so no non-vacuous
comparison against a matched byte arm is possible. Settling the question requires a
richer equivalence resource:

1. **Real paraphrase data** — sentence pairs with graded equivalence, so positives
   span many motifs rather than four.
2. **Test-time compilation at the instance level** — compile per-task from the
   evaluator's own demonstration pairs, where the motif set is set by the task, not
   by a hand-written 5-entry table.
3. **A learned equivalence head** — explicitly out of scope under the
   MINIMAL-TRAINING mandate without `M5` approval.

Option 2 is the one the zero-pretraining invariant actually implies, and it remains
**unbuilt**.
